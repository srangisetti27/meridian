The sidebar is the part I'm proudest of, and it never changes: the tool's own health checks, its own known problems, and its own rulebook — permanently on screen. Nothing about this product asks to be trusted; it shows its working.

3 · Anatomy of one answer card

Every answer has the same seven parts, in the same order. Learn this once and every sample below reads instantly:

  1. The routing line (top-left, small caps) — what the question was interpreted as, and what it matched on: a suggested question, a keyword, a rep's name, or "LLM classification, validated against the approved intent catalog." Misunderstandings are visible here, never hidden.
  2. The trust badge (top-right) — Verified Verified with assumptions or Insufficient data. Fixed rules, not feelings.
  3. The answer — two to four sentences. With AI on, it's the model's phrasing that survived validation; otherwise the engine's own wording. Below it, the verification line: "AI summary · N numbers verified against computed results."
  4. Metric tiles — the key figures as cards.
  5. A chart, when one helps.
  6. Data-quality warnings — always visible, orange-edged, quantified in dollars.
  7. Three tabs — Source data (the actual rows, with problem rows tagged), Calculation (numerator, denominator, filters, thresholds, written out), Assumptions.

Each new answer also fires a small toast, bottom-right: "Answer verified — N numbers checked against the engine."

14 · What changes when the AI is on

Everything above is the deterministic engine — it's what you get with the sidebar toggle off, and it's the exact material the AI is allowed to rephrase. With the toggle on, three things change (and only three):

1. Conversational phrasings route correctly

Type "Are we going to make the number this quarter?" — keyword rules alone can't place that, so the model picks from the menu. The routing line shows it: "Company pipeline summary · LLM classification, validated against the approved intent catalog." Same numbers as chapter 12 — the model only chose the menu item.

2. Answers read more naturally — with a proof line

The headline becomes the model's phrasing of the same computed package, and beneath it: "AI summary · 11 numbers verified against computed results · deterministic wording under Calculation." If the model had included even one number the engine didn't produce — even "$3.65M" as shorthand — the entire summary would be rejected, the engine's wording shown instead, and the rejection displayed. The guardrail is visible either way.

3. The toast confirms each check

Bottom-right after every answer: "Answer verified — N numbers checked against the engine."

And if the AI provider goes down mid-demo? Flip the toggle — or don't even bother: the app degrades to exactly what you've seen in this document, automatically. We watched that happen during a real provider outage. The AI is the polish; the trust is in the machine underneath.
